## Purpose and limits

This controlled generic SWMS provides a **daily selection and briefing framework** for ecological field surveys. Staff must complete the project details, activity description, site conditions, emergency arrangements and team acknowledgement. They must then select every relevant specialist hazard module and use the current controlled source SWMS alongside this document.

> This form does **not** replace a selected source SWMS, the site-specific Emergency Response Plan, a Principal Contractor control, a permit, an induction, a Traffic Management Plan, a Traffic Guidance Scheme or any required authorisation. It does not authorise construction, plant operation, traffic control, electrical work, asbestos disturbance, confined-space rescue, diving or other excluded work.

Where a hazard cannot be eliminated or adequately controlled, work must not proceed. Every worker may stop work. The Field Lead must move the team to a safe location, notify the Principal Contractor or site supervisor where applicable, and re-brief controls before recommencement.

## Daily completion requirements

The staff portal form must record the project, client, job number, date, exact survey activity, site, approved access and egress route, Field Lead, Principal Contractor or site supervisor, first aider, communications roles, Emergency Response Plan reference, muster point, coordinates, nearest responder access and check-in arrangements. The Field Lead must also record the daily review of weather, visibility, ground and bank stability, water conditions, traffic and plant operations, electrical assets, asbestos information, permits, authorisations and communications.

Each crew member must be listed with their role and acknowledge the daily briefing. The Field Lead must confirm that the generic controls, current controlled source documents, selected controls and stop-work triggers are available and have been briefed before signing.

## Generic controls applying to all selected work

1. Limit the work to ecological survey and observation within the approved project scope. Use remote observation, existing information, cameras, binoculars, drones, torches, borescopes, extendable equipment or another safer method wherever practicable.
2. Before mobilisation and again at the daily pre-start or toolbox talk, confirm the work area, access route, roles, site-specific Emergency Response Plan, communication plan, task equipment, emergency equipment and applicable Principal Contractor controls.
3. Confirm that every worker is fit, competent, trained and authorised for the selected work. Verify current mandatory credentials and selected PPE, first-aid, communication and rescue equipment. Tag out faulty equipment.
4. Maintain the required buddy, standby, visual contact, check-in, separation and supervision arrangements. Reassess if weather, location, operations or the survey method changes.
5. Stop work, move to the nominated safe location or muster point, notify the Field Lead and Principal Contractor, and do not recommence until the risk is controlled and communicated.
6. In an emergency, make the area safe for responders. Call 000 or 112, or activate InReach or PLB where required. Provide location and access information, account for personnel, report the event, review the source SWMS and Emergency Response Plan, and restock used equipment.

## Selected-control and stop-work record

For every selected module, the Field Lead must record **why the module applies**, the exact task and location, the source document revision, and confirmation that the module’s controls, authorisations, training, PPE, equipment and stop-work triggers have been briefed. The electronic staff form displays the relevant selected controls and stop-work triggers beside the selection.

The form must not be used to change, reduce or override a source module’s scope limits, exclusions, Principal Contractor dependencies, training conditions, emergency response sequence or cross-references. Where more than one trigger applies, staff must select and apply every relevant module.

## Stop-work and emergency boundaries

Work must stop immediately if a selected module identifies a trigger, including unstable ground or edges; unsafe water, weather or visibility; failed atmosphere, ventilation or communication; unclear permits, asset locations or exclusion zones; failed traffic or plant separation; suspected asbestos; tunnel instability; or any worker feeling unsafe. The team must move to the selected module’s safe location, retreat point or muster point and may not recommence until the risk is controlled and communicated.

For an emergency, the team must use the site-specific Emergency Response Plan. Ecology Consulting personnel must not attempt confined-space or tunnel rescue. Water rescue is reach-or-throw only unless a person is trained and authorised. For electrical contact, staff must remain clear and not approach a person, plant or vegetation in contact with a conductor until the area is confirmed de-energised and safe.

## Field Lead verification

The Field Lead signs the completed electronic form to confirm that the team has been briefed, current controlled source documents are available, all relevant hazards have been selected, project-specific controls are in place, emergency arrangements are understood and the activity is authorised to proceed only within the stated limits.
