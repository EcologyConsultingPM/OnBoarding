🔴 1. NSW Updated BAM — consultation remains open

The updated Biodiversity Assessment Method (BAM) was released for public consultation on 10 September 2026. Consultation closes 11:59 pm, 10 October 2026. The changes respond to amendments to the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016, proposed changes to the Biodiversity Conservation Regulation 2017, and recommendations from the BAM five-year review.

Importantly for project work, a 12-month transitional period applies from publication of the updated BAM. During the transition, an assessor can certify a BAR prepared under either BAM 2020 or the updated BAM.

Ecology Consulting action:

Do not treat the updated BAM as simply replacing BAM 2020 immediately.
For current and upcoming BAR projects, record which BAM version is being used.
Senior Ecologist/BAM-accredited assessor to determine the appropriate methodology for each project.
Monitor the consultation outcome and final BAM.

🔴 2. NSW Native Vegetation Regulatory Map — now operational

The NSW Native Vegetation Regulatory Map was published on 31 August 2026 and is now an important desktop consideration for rural NSW projects involving native vegetation clearing. It identifies land categories under Part 5A of the Local Land Services Act 2013.

The department has specifically stated that accredited assessors can use the map's land categorisation when considering Biodiversity Offsets Scheme entry thresholds and application of the BAM.

Ecology Consulting action:

Add a mandatory NVR Map desktop check to relevant NSW project workflows where rural land and native vegetation clearing are involved.

Record:

map category
date checked
mapping source/version
relevant lot/property
any mapping uncertainty
whether a map review is required.

The Bland and Temora LGAs remain subject to transitional mapping arrangements.

🟠 3. Commonwealth EPBC reforms — implementation accelerating

The Commonwealth environmental reforms continue moving toward the next implementation stage.

The National Environmental Protection Agency commenced on 1 July 2026, and four National Environmental Standards were made in August:

Matters of National Environmental Significance
Environmental Offsets
Community Engagement
Data and Information

The standards do not yet apply to assessment and approval decisions under the new approval tests; those changes commence on or before 1 December 2026.

A further consultation on regulations supporting the next stage of reform is open until 9 pm AEDT, 13 October 2026.

Ecology Consulting action:

For Commonwealth/referral projects:

continue using the applicable current EPBC requirements;
don't assume the new approval system is already operational;
identify projects likely to still be progressing around 1 December 2026;
monitor transitional arrangements;
update the internal EPBC/pathway training module as implementation details are finalised.

🟡 4. ACT — Nature Conservation Act remains an emerging change area

The current ACT Nature Conservation Act 2014 remains in force. The ACT legislation register currently identifies proposed amendments associated with the Urban Forest and Planning Legislation Amendment Bill 2026, while the current Act remains based on its 26 December 2025 republication.

The broader Nature Conservation Act review/rewrite remains relevant to monitoring, but I did not identify a new ACT-wide biodiversity assessment methodology or major threatened-species survey requirement commencing specifically during this week's review period.

Ecology Consulting action:

For ACT projects:

continue using the current legislative framework;
don't incorporate proposed amendments as current requirements until commenced;
continue monitoring the Nature Conservation Act review and associated legislative changes.
