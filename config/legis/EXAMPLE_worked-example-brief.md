# NSW/ACT Ecology Brief — Worked Example

**This is a reference issue, not a live brief.** It exists to show the expected standard:
how status labels are applied, how dates are separated, how a conflict between sources is
surfaced rather than smoothed, and what an "action" looks like.

Read it before your first `/weekly-brief` run. Delete or keep as you prefer — the scanner
ignores files that do not match `YYYY-MM-DD_nsw-act-ecology-brief.md`.

## 1. Bottom line

The dominant issue for the practice is not a NSW change — it is the **staged Commonwealth
rollout**, where the National Environmental Standards exist as published documents but do not
yet govern assessment and approval decisions. Treating them as current criteria is the most
likely way to mis-scope a federal referral this quarter. In NSW, the position is stable: the
"genuine measures" avoid/minimise provisions of the 2024 Amendment Act remain uncommenced
pending supporting regulations, so BDARs are still prepared under the pre-existing standard.

**Overdue register items:** 4 — see §6.

## 2. Developments

### D1 — EPBC National Environmental Standards: published, but not yet decision criteria

| | |
|---|---|
| **Jurisdiction** | Commonwealth |
| **Status** | `ADOPTED POLICY / GUIDELINE` — but see "Applies from" |
| **Severity** | HIGH |
| **Published** | First four Standards available; reforms commenced 24 August 2026 for some uses |
| **Commences / effective** | Staged |
| **Applies from** | **On or before 1 December 2026** for assessment and approval decisions |
| **Triggers fired** | T01 (scope/fee), T08 (EPBC strategy), T10 (approval timeframe) |

**What changed.** DCCEEW states that the first four Standards are now available, and — this is
the operative sentence — that these "will not apply to assessments and approval decisions
until the new approval tests commence", which "will happen on or before 1 December 2026". The
department separately records that a set of reforms "commenced on 24 August 2026 for some
uses" and "will apply to referral and approval decisions by 1 December".

**Why this is the trap.** "Standard published" and "Standard governs your referral" are
different events separated by months. A referral lodged now is not assessed against the
Standards; a referral lodged after the trigger date is. That is a strategic timing question,
not a documentation question.

**Transitional treatment.** The phrase "for some uses" is doing real work and is not fully
resolved on the department's page. Do not assume a clean before/after line. Where a client's
decision turns on it, verify the specific provision on the Federal Register of Legislation
before advising.

**Projects affected.** Any project with a live or contemplated EPBC referral, particularly
those where the outcome depends on offsets — the reform package replaces the "no net loss"
test with a net gain test and introduces an "unacceptable impact" test for which offsets are
not available as a remedy.

**Why it matters commercially.** Two distinct exposures. First, referral timing is now a
strategic variable a client may reasonably expect advice on. Second, fixed-fee federal
assessment work spanning the trigger date carries genuine scope risk, because the assessment
criteria may change mid-engagement.

**Sources.**
- Primary: https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/epbc/epbc-act-reform/stronger-environmental-protection-restoration (accessed 2026-09-09)
- Primary (rollout hub): https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/epbc/epbc-act-reform (accessed 2026-09-09)
- Cross-check (tier 5, interpretation only): King & Wood Mallesons, "Mark your calendars: EPBC Act Reform commencement through 2026 and beyond"

**Conflict noted.** Secondary commentary is inconsistent on the passage date — some sources
say the Reform Bills passed 28 November 2025, others 27 November 2025, and Royal Assent is
separately reported as 1 December 2025 with Part 19B commencing 2 December 2025. These are
reconcilable (Senate vote, House concurrence, assent, commencement are four different events)
but they are frequently collapsed in commentary. **For any advice that turns on a date, use
the Federal Register of Legislation compilation, not an alert.** I have not treated the
passage date as material here because nothing operational hangs on it.

**Action.**
- [ ] Add an EPBC timing question to the federal-pathway scoping checklist: does the client
      benefit from referring before, or after, the Standards apply?
- [ ] Insert a scope-change clause into fixed-fee federal proposals spanning 1 December 2026.
- [ ] Verify the "commenced for some uses" scope on the Federal Register before the next
      federal referral is lodged. **Do not advise from the summary page alone.**

### D2 — NSW avoid/minimise "genuine measures" provisions remain uncommenced

| | |
|---|---|
| **Jurisdiction** | NSW |
| **Status** | `MADE, NOT COMMENCED` |
| **Severity** | HIGH (as a *restraint* on practice, not a new requirement) |
| **Published** | Amendment Act passed 22 November 2024 |
| **Commences / effective** | Most provisions commenced **7 March 2025**; the genuine-measures provisions did **not** |
| **Applies from** | Not yet — awaiting BC Regulation amendments |
| **Triggers fired** | T06 (avoid/minimise evidence), T02 (BDAR content) |

**What changed.** Nothing this week — and that is the point of reporting it. DCCEEW's position
remains that on 7 March 2025 all provisions in the Amendment Act commenced "except those
relating to the assessment of genuine measures to avoid and minimise impacts to biodiversity",
with amendments to the Biodiversity Conservation Regulation 2017 still under development.

**Why it is in Developments rather than Watchlist.** Because the live risk is *over*-compliance
driven by commentary. Multiple law-firm alerts describe the strengthened avoid/minimise
requirements in the present tense. An assessor who redesigns a BDAR methodology around a
requirement that has not commenced adds unrecoverable cost and invites an inconsistency
finding at review.

**Transitional treatment.** Not applicable while uncommenced. When the regulation is made,
transitional treatment of in-progress BDARs becomes the critical question — flag it then.

**Why it matters commercially.** Prepare, do not deploy. The work of building a stronger
avoid/minimise evidence trail is worth doing now because it will be required; presenting it as
a current statutory requirement to a client is wrong and may be unbillable.

**Sources.**
- Primary: https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/topics/animals-and-plants/biodiversity-offsets-scheme/news-and-updates/biodiversity-offsets-scheme-reforms (accessed 2026-09-09)
- Primary: DCCEEW, "Reform summary and commencement impacts — BOS Amendment Act" (PDF),
  https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/reform-summary-and-commencement-impacts-bos-amendment-act.pdf (accessed 2026-09-09)

**Action.**
- [ ] Draft the enhanced avoid/minimise evidence section as a **template variant**, held

## 7. Confidence & gaps

**Confidence in this brief:** MEDIUM. The two Development items rest on official agency pages
read directly, which is adequate for awareness but not for advice on a specific referral.

**Where sources conflicted.**
- *EPBC passage and commencement dates.* Commentary variously reports 27 and 28 November 2025
  for passage, with assent 1 December 2025 and Part 19B commencing 2 December 2025. These
  describe different events and are not truly contradictory, but they are routinely conflated.
  I prefer the legislation register over all commentary on any date question. Nothing
  operational in this issue turns on the passage date.
- *Whether the Standards currently bite.* Some secondary commentary discusses the Standards in
  terms that imply present application. The department's own page states they do not apply to
  assessment and approval decisions until the new approval tests commence. **The departmental
  statement is more credible**: it is the administering agency, it is more recent, and it
  addresses the question directly rather than in passing.

**Where coverage was thin.**
- The precise scope of "commenced on 24 August 2026 for some uses" is not resolved from the
  departmental page alone. This is a real gap and it is the one most likely to matter.
- NSW BAM-C release notes and vegetation integrity benchmark updates were not opened
  individually in this reference issue.

**What I could not check.**
- Council-level changes. No LGA sweep was performed for this worked example.
- BOS Update newsletter PDFs for the window were not individually opened.

**What remains uncertain.**
- When the BC Regulation amendments will be made, and how they will treat BDARs already in
  preparation. This is the single most consequential unknown for NSW BDAR work.
- Whether NSW will make consequential amendments to align the BC Act regime with the reformed
  EPBC Act, and on what timetable.

**Known blind spots in this method.**
- Council changes are only swept for active LGAs.
- Changes communicated only via the assessor portal, direct agency email or closed briefings
  will not be detected until they surface publicly.
- Emerging practice is anecdotal by nature and is labelled as such.
